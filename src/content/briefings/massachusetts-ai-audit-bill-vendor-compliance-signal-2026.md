Massachusetts is advancing [S.3178](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3178), an economic-development measure with AI safety provisions targeting frontier developers. Under the proposal, covered developers would hire independent evaluators to assess their models for catastrophic risks every four months. Anthropic supports the measure; OpenAI and Google oppose it, preferring annual audits. The bill remains in conference committee and may change before passage. For buyers, the vendor split is a prompt to ask how outside reviews could affect release schedules and pricing.

**Key takeaways**

- [S.3178](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3178) would require independent safety evaluations of frontier models every 120 days, per The Information's September 3 reporting.
- The bill targets "large frontier developers"; specific revenue and research-spend thresholds have not been independently confirmed from primary bill text.
- Anthropic supports the bill; OpenAI and Google oppose it, preferring annual audits (the Illinois model).
- [84 new AI laws enacted in 27 states](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/tcai-report-84-new-ai-laws-enacted-in-27-states-in-2026) in 2026; Congress failed to pass federal AI preemption in 2025.
- The bill has not passed; enterprise operators have no compliance obligation today.

## What Does Massachusetts S.3178 Actually Require?

The bill passed the Massachusetts Senate in July 2026 inside a broader economic development package. A [conference committee was appointed July 20](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-september4-2026) and is still deliberating.

The AI provisions target "large frontier developers" and, per [The Information's September 3 report](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-splits-google-openai-state-ai-safety-bill) (paywalled) confirmed by [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-breaks-with-peers-on-massachusetts-ai-safety-bill/), would require independent evaluations every four months. Specific revenue or research-spend eligibility thresholds were not confirmed from bill text available to AdvancedAI at publication. Most enterprise operators are not the direct target — their vendors are — but model-release delays from a failed evaluation flow downstream to buyers.

## What Does the Vendor Split Signal for Procurement?

Anthropic's head of US state and local government relations [wrote on X](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-breaks-with-peers-on-massachusetts-ai-safety-bill/) that S.3178 "raises the bar on AI safety while ensuring innovation continues." Safety organizations Encode and Secure AI Project co-signed. Operators should weigh that a vendor publicly backing a requirement it may already meet could have competitive as well as safety reasons for doing so — treat vendor safety endorsements as useful signal, not independent validation.

OpenAI [proposed the Illinois model instead](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/02/how-silicon-valley-lost-its-war-against-state-ai-laws-01054133) — annual third-party audits — and Google's position aligns. Ask each AI vendor directly: do you currently undergo independent safety evaluations, and how would mandatory quarterly reviews affect your model release timeline?

## What Operators Should Do Now

The bill has not passed. Enterprise buyers have no compliance obligation today. Add one question to your next vendor conversation: **Do you currently undergo independent safety evaluations, and how would mandatory quarterly state audits affect your release timeline?**

Compare answers on current evaluation cadence, release-gating criteria, customer notification, and likely pricing effects rather than treating a vendor's public position as proof of readiness. [OpenAI's Daybreak program](/briefings/openai-daybreak-ibm-enterprise-cyber-2026/) is an early example of how evaluation status already gates enterprise access — mandatory quarterly audits add another potential layer. Operators who [reviewed vendor handling of mandatory government safety reviews](/briefings/gpt-56-government-vetting-enterprise-model-access-2026/) found readiness varied far more than marketing suggested.

Watch for: the Massachusetts conference committee vote; Governor Healey's signature; and whether other states adopt the Illinois or Massachusetts audit cadence in 2027.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Massachusetts S.3178 require enterprise operators to do anything?

No. The bill targets "large frontier developers," not enterprise buyers. Operators have no compliance obligation under the current text. Model-release delays or added vendor costs from a failed evaluation could affect pricing and availability downstream, but no customer-facing requirement exists today.

### What is the difference between the Massachusetts and Illinois approaches?

Illinois requires annual independent safety audits. Massachusetts S.3178 would require evaluations every 120 days — four times per year. OpenAI and Google back the Illinois cadence; Anthropic supports Massachusetts. Which approach states adopt more broadly will shape the near-term vendor governance baseline.

### Why would Anthropic support regulation that OpenAI and Google oppose?

Anthropic states S.3178 "raises the bar on AI safety while ensuring innovation continues." Whether that support also reflects competitive considerations — vendors differ in their current evaluation cadences — is plausible but not established by public reporting. Weigh vendor safety endorsements alongside what those vendors' commercial incentives might be.
